## How it reads

- With a date on the X axis, the chart summarizes rows into periods. Choose the interval, the week start, and the metric: a count, or a summary of a measure.
- **Display** draws a line between period values, an area filled to zero, or stacked areas that add nonnegative counts or sums across series.
- **Missing periods** either leave a gap, or draw an empty count or sum at zero. An average needs observations, so an empty period has no average to draw.
- Each series can use the right axis, its own line style, and points.

## Annotations

The **Notes** tab marks what the lines mean:

- A **line** at a Y value marks a target. On a date axis, a line at a date marks an event, such as a release.
- A **band** shades a span of Y values, such as a tolerance, or a period of dates or X values. **Bracket** marks the span along the top edge instead of shading the plot.
- A **callout** labels one row of a line chart by its key and the series it sits on. A chart that sums rows into periods has no single rows, so its callouts sit at a fixed date and value.
- An **interval** shades between lower and upper bounds that each row already holds, such as a supplied forecast range. Its **Meaning** says what the bounds are; the chart computes no interval itself. Rows whose lower bound is above the upper bound are drawn with the bounds swapped.

On a chart with a right axis, choose which axis a value reads on. The product activity example marks the release period and a 4% target; the calendar example brackets a summer sale.
