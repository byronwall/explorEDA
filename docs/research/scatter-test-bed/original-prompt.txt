### 5. Mahalanobis distance and ellipses

Implement point inspection with both squared distance `D²` and distance `D`, each clearly named.
Offer distance coloring or highlighting, nested distance contours, and an inspectable table of the most distant rows.
State which population estimates the center and covariance, and which population receives distance scores.
Keep the reference population visible when a selection changes.

Implement two distinct ellipse modes:

- **Data ellipse:** a contour of fitted Mahalanobis distance around the estimated center.
- **Confidence region for the mean:** a finite-sample region for the unknown two-dimensional population mean.

Do not use the generic label “confidence ellipse” for both.
Prediction regions for future observations and statistical tolerance regions are separate methods. Document or defer them explicitly.

For the data ellipse, start from `D² = (z - mean)' S^-1 (z - mean)`.
With a bivariate Gaussian model, a fitted coverage contour commonly uses the chi-square threshold with two degrees of freedom.
For coverage `a`, that threshold is `q = -2 ln(1 - a)`; the ellipse semiaxes are `sqrt(q * eigenvalue)`.
At 95%, `q` is approximately `5.991464547` and the distance radius is approximately `2.447746831`.
Label this as fitted Gaussian coverage. It is not exact finite-sample outlier significance.
Do not substitute the one-dimensional `1.96` multiplier or equate two standard deviations with 95% bivariate coverage.

For the mean region, verify the Hotelling result for independent bivariate normal observations and `n > 2`.
The boundary uses `n * (mean - mu)' S^-1 (mean - mu) <= [2(n - 1)/(n - 2)] F(2, n - 2; a)`.
Its semiaxes therefore use the threshold divided by `n`.
Verify the quantile calculation independently before shipping this mode. State its assumptions beside the method explanation.
[NIST's Hotelling T-squared reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section5/pmc543.htm) supplies the finite-sample scaling.

Handle too few pairs, zero variance, identical points, collinearity, and nearly singular covariance explicitly.
Use a scale-aware numerical check. Do not silently add a ridge or use a pseudoinverse while keeping ordinary coverage labels.
Do not label every distant row an error. Distance is a diagnostic under a stated reference model.
