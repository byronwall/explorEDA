# Find a paid use for explorEDA

## My read

Byron prefers a recurring SaaS business around explorEDA to selling audits. The likely route is a focused product around the component. It should serve a specific customer, connect to useful data, and support a task worth paying for. Whether that route beats selling the component remains a hypothesis.

The initial request covered 50–100 opportunities and their data, integration, surrounding application, and component needs. Shopify and DevOps now deserve particular attention. Neither is a selected market, and no specific product has been approved.

That research now exists. The immediate need is to make it useful without requiring a single reading of a very large report. Byron wants the source files preserved in this initiative, with a short summary and likely next steps. He expects to return to the full material over time.

The current task is a shortlist of ten recurring SaaS ideas, with a concrete account of the surrounding application. The longer-term outcome is a reasoned product choice and a small test of repeat value. The shortlist does not approve prices or start a build.

## What matters most

- Find a paid customer task, including the action that follows analysis.
- Understand data access, integration work, the surrounding app, and actual component gaps.
- Keep every original idea and source reference available for later review.
- Separate Byron's goals from Pro's assumptions, rankings, and proposed experiments.
- Make the next reading session small enough to complete.
- Prefer continuing software value over a sequence of manually delivered reports.

## The intended experience

Byron reads the SaaS shortlist, then compares one commerce idea with one developer-tool idea. Each explains the recurring customer task, data route, supporting app, and component changes. The original family research remains available when a detail matters.

As preferences emerge, this initiative records the chosen direction and why it changed. The original research stays unchanged. A selected product can receive its own shape and plan when its buyer, outcome, and scope are clear.

## Boundaries

Preserve the complete report, catalogue, and original archive. Keep interpretation in separate helper documents. Retain rejected and uncertain ideas because future customer access or preferences could change their value.

Do not turn this catalogue into 75 projects or a component feature backlog. Do not treat a documented API as proof of usable customer data. Do not treat a proposed monthly price as evidence of repeat demand.

Pro favored quick paid pilots, often delivered as assisted reviews. Byron's later preference changes the ranking criterion to recurring software value. A manually supplied sample can still test a calculation. It does not turn the business into an audit service. Customer acquisition and support work remain part of the choice.

## What seems settled

Recurring SaaS is the preferred business model. Shopify and DevOps are priority areas for comparison. The component supplies the analysis workspace; the product needs a customer-specific purpose and supporting workflow. Commercial claims remain untested.

This request covers research intake, digestion, and a SaaS shortlist. It does not authorize outreach, purchases, connector installation, product implementation, or publication.

## Current reality that matters

Pro delivered 75 opportunities across 12 families. Its labels are 29 candidates for validation, 32 with unresolved dependencies, and 14 rejected first-product proposals. These are research judgments, not customer evidence.

The report used a newer repository revision than the intake checkout. The SaaS comparison rechecked selected official platform and competitor documentation. These checks establish available features, not customer demand. Future shaping must recheck the selected product's data and component requirements.
