## How it reads

<ReadingGuide chart="donut">
  <Part at="slice">
    Each slice is one category's share of every row that passes the other
    charts' filters, so the shares always sum to 100%.
  </Part>
  <Part at="center">
    The center shows the whole: the row count, or the sum of the measure.
  </Part>
  <Part at="order">Slices run largest first, clockwise from the top.</Part>
  <Part at="legend">
    The legend lists each category with its share, in the slices' colors.
  </Part>
  <Part at="other">
    **Top N** (six by default, since thin slices are hard to compare) sets how
    many categories get a slice; the rest pool into **Other**.
  </Part>
  <Part>
    With a color field and scale, slices take the same colors as other charts
    that color by that field.
  </Part>
</ReadingGuide>

## When it won't draw

A share needs a whole made of nonnegative parts. If a category's sum is negative, or the values sum to zero, the chart says so instead of drawing a ring. For values that can be negative, such as profit, use a bar chart or a waterfall.

## Select and trace

- Click a slice or its legend entry to select that category. Click **Other** to select every category it pools.
- Click empty space to clear the selection.
- Alt-click a slice to see its value, its share of the whole, and its source rows. For Other, the trace also lists the categories it pools.

## Donut or bars?

A donut suits a few categories of one whole. To compare many categories, or the same mix across groups, use bars: the example sets a donut beside 100% bars of the same channel mix in each region.
