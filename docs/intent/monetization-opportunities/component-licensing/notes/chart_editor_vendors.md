## Key question 1: Highcharts (Highsoft AS)

### Takeaway
Highcharts sells per-developer-seat licenses in Internal, SaaS, SaaS+ and OEM scopes, either perpetual (with a first-year "Advantage" maintenance plan that auto-renews) or annual; enforcement is contractual (audit rights, non-compliance fees) backed by a soft console message from a software key rather than a hard runtime lock. The company is a ~60-person Norwegian firm with 2024 revenue of about NOK 101M, built almost entirely on export sales.

### Cited Findings
- Webshop seat prices (checked 2026-10-10): Highcharts Core $366 per seat (required base), Stock +$366/seat, Maps +$128/seat, Gantt +$73/seat, Highcharts for Python +$110/yr, Dashboards $264/seat, Grid Pro $316/seat — [Highcharts Shop](https://shop.highcharts.com/)
- License types: Internal License (internal apps and private/intranet websites on servers the licensee controls, not public websites); SaaS License (formerly "Developer License": Internal scope plus one External Application; public websites count as External Applications); SaaS+ (up to five External Applications, no price listed); OEM (products distributed to or hosted for customers; quote only, priced by named products, developer seats and "customer installations") — [Highcharts Shop](https://shop.highcharts.com/)
- Developer seat definition: "anyone simultaneously working with the API or source code"; webshop sells up to six seats for Internal and SaaS, more requires a quote; Dashboards seats are counted separately and edit-mode-only users are not developers — [Highcharts Shop](https://shop.highcharts.com/)
- Perpetual licenses are usage "in perpetuity", include Advantage for the first year, then renew yearly until cancelled, and cannot be downgraded; Annual licenses require usage to stop when the subscription ends; Annual is available for Internal, SaaS and SaaS+ — [Highcharts Shop](https://shop.highcharts.com/)
- Advantage support tiers: Advantage 10 support hours per developer, 36-hour initial response; Advantage Plus 20 hours per developer, 17-hour response; prices not shown — [Highcharts Shop](https://shop.highcharts.com/)
- Affiliate use and deployment on third-party premises are excluded from Internal and SaaS and require a customized/OEM license; startup discounts exist but no percentage is given — [Highcharts Shop](https://shop.highcharts.com/)
- License agreement section 3.2.5: a software key is provided on purchase but "the supply of a software key by Highsoft is not itself a license grant"; a missing or invalid key may display a console message, which "will not in any way limit the functionality of the Software" — [Highcharts License Agreement](https://shop.highcharts.com/license)
- Enforcement is contractual: Highsoft may audit once per year during the term and once in the five years after (10 days' notice, Highsoft pays costs if no discrepancy), may require retrospective purchase plus a "Non-Compliance Fee" for unlicensed use, may invoice excess use and recover investigation costs, and may rate-limit or block CDN use — [Highcharts License Agreement](https://shop.highcharts.com/license)
- Highsoft support states there is "no mandatory license key associated with the software" and "no activation is needed"; the license "is solely a legal document"; evaluation is free "for testing and demonstration, but once you go live with your product, a commercial license is required" (search snippet of support.highcharts.com; the host failed to resolve on 2026-10-10) — [Highcharts support: trial article](https://support.highcharts.com/support/solutions/articles/44002332705-do-you-have-a-demo-or-trial-of-your-software-)
- Highcharts Grid Pro v3.0 is the exception: it "requires a valid Grid Key in deployed environments" and writes a browser console warning when the key is missing, invalid, or expired, without blocking the grid — [ComponentSource Grid Pro licensing](https://www.componentsource.com/es/product/highcharts-grid/licensing)
- Non-commercial terms: the commercial agreement says non-commercial use "authorized by Highsoft" is "governed by separate terms for non-commercial usage"; Creative Commons is not mentioned in the commercial agreement — [Highcharts License Agreement](https://shop.highcharts.com/license)
- The non-commercial EULA grants use for "Personal Use and Educational Use only", defines personal use as entirely non-commercial, non-professional use by a natural person, and explicitly excludes use "in connection with any business, trade, profession, government entity, non-profit organization, or any other organizational context" — [Highcharts non-commercial EULA](https://shop.highcharts.com/license-eula); older marketing text (quoted on G2) said non-profits, personal websites and school projects get a CC Attribution-NonCommercial license — [G2 Highcharts pricing page](https://www.g2.com/products/highcharts/pricing). These conflict; the EULA is the newer controlling text.
- Under the non-commercial license "you must retain the Highcharts watermark"; the software "is not licensed as an open source software" and is not compatible with Apache 2 or GPL — [search snippet, Highcharts FAQ via G2](https://www.g2.com/products/highcharts/pricing)
- Education: free for coursework, classroom teaching and non-funded academic research; excludes administrative, operational and commercially funded research; no direct support — [Highcharts for Education](https://www.highcharts.com/education/)
- Company: Highsoft AS, Sentrumsgata 44, 6893 Vik i Sogn, Norway — [Highcharts About](https://www.highcharts.com/about/); founder Torstein Hønsi; "99% of Highsoft's revenue comes from overseas" — [Media City Bergen, Villsauen 2022 award](https://mediacitybergen.no/home/highsoft-won-the-villsauen-2022-award)
- 2024 accounts (NOK, registry aggregator): revenue 101,386,000; operating result 6,858,000; pre-tax 8,859,000; net 6,931,000; 60 employees; Highsoft Holding AS owns 91% — [1881.no company record](https://www.1881.no/data-programvare-og-utvikling/data-programvare-og-utvikling-vestland/data-programvare-og-utvikling-vik-i-sogn/highsoft-as_103516954S2/); corroborated by [Purehelp](https://www.purehelp.no/m/company/view/highsoftholdingas/934991923)
- Historical sales signal: all-time-high month October 2014 with $640,000 of licenses sold in one month — [Highcharts blog post 5343](https://www.highcharts.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/5343)
- Third-party estimates disagree: Prospeo estimates ~$6.7M revenue and 51-100 employees; AroundDeal and RankMyAI list 11-50 employees — [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/highsoft); [AroundDeal](https://www.arounddeal.com/c/highsoft-as/f4kvfnycbm)
- GitHub highcharts/highcharts: 12,493 stars, license "NOASSERTION" (custom), created 2010-06-11; npm `highcharts` 11,933,600 downloads last month, 2,632,694 last week — GitHub and npm APIs, 2026-10-10

### Inferences
- The "honor system" characterization is accurate for the core library: the key is a soft console nag, and the real deterrent is the audit clause plus the fact that large enterprises will not ship unlicensed code. The newer Grid Pro product shows Highsoft moving toward runtime key validation for new products.
- NOK 101M at ~60 staff is roughly $9-10M revenue, about $150-170k per employee, consistent with a mature, profitable, bootstrapped component vendor rather than a VC-scale company.
- Highcharts' pricing is additive (base Core seat plus per-product add-ons per seat), which lets the list price stay low ($366) while the realistic Stock+Maps bundle for a SaaS team of 3 runs well over $2,500 before Advantage renewals.

### Gaps
- Webshop did not show Annual subscription prices, SaaS+ prices, Advantage/Advantage Plus renewal prices, or OEM prices (quote only).
- No official statement of trial duration; the support site did not resolve during this check.
- Exact non-profit status is ambiguous between the older CC-BY-NC marketing text and the current EULA.

## Key question 2: amCharts

### Takeaway
amCharts gives away the full library under a freeware license with a small logo in the chart corner and sells per-developer-seat licenses (Basic, Single App/SaaS, OEM, Extended OEM) that remove the branding, as either an annual subscription or a perpetual one-time price; the company is a very small distributed team in Vilnius.

### Cited Findings
- "You can download and use all amCharts 5 products for free"; "the only limitation ... is that a small amCharts logo will be displayed in the corner of your charts"; removing the logo requires a commercial license — [amCharts Download](https://www.amcharts.com/download/)
- npm package note: "if you have a commercial amCharts 5 license, this software is covered by your license, which supersedes any other license bundled with this package. Otherwise, the use of this software is covered by a freeware license" — [npm @amcharts/amcharts5](https://npmjs.com/package/@amcharts/amcharts5)
- Price table (checked 2026-10-10), per developer seat, USD: Charts Basic $80/yr or $180 perpetual (support not included; add-on $190/seat/yr); Single App or SaaS website $280/yr or $650 perpetual (first year support included, then $190/seat/yr); OEM $1,200/yr or $2,700 perpetual (then $790/seat/yr); Extended OEM contact sales (90 support hours/seat). Maps, Stock Chart and Gantt are 20% lower: Basic $64/$144, SaaS $224/$520, OEM $960/$2,160 — [amCharts Online Store](https://www.amcharts.com/online-store/)
- Annual "renews once every 12 months"; Perpetual is a one-time purchase that does not expire. Free minor upgrades on all licenses; free major upgrades while the subscription (amPlus) is active — [amCharts Online Store](https://www.amcharts.com/online-store/)
- Usage limits: Basic allows unlimited public websites with no login but not SaaS (login-required) sites or redistributable apps; Single App/SaaS adds unlimited SaaS websites; OEM adds redistributable mobile/desktop/web apps; Extended OEM adds use in development tools, by affiliates and in third-party rebranded software. All tiers: unlimited users, pages, servers, dev/test environments, subdomains; 1 developer per seat — [amCharts Online Store](https://www.amcharts.com/online-store/); [Licenses explained](https://www.amcharts.com/online-store/licenses-explained/)
- "Removable amCharts branding" and source code are included on all paid tiers; any license can be upgraded by paying the retail difference; large seat counts trigger a contact-sales prompt — [amCharts Online Store](https://www.amcharts.com/online-store/)
- Reseller fact sheet: "The Free License includes the same functionality as the paid licenses, but will include an amCharts watermark on each chart"; all amCharts 5 licenses are perpetual for the current major version — [ComponentSource amCharts fact sheet](https://www.componentsource.com/product/amcharts/factsheet)
- Company: based in Vilnius, Lithuania; founder Antanas Marcelionis built amMap in 2004 as a side project and left his job in 2006 — [amCharts history](https://www.amcharts.com/?p=79); headcount estimates: ZoomInfo 1-10 employees, an older Handsontable case study "distributed team of 13 people" — [ZoomInfo](https://zoominfo.com/c/amcharts/353359427); [Handsontable customer story](https://handsontable.com/customers/amcharts)
- GitHub amcharts/amcharts5: 446 stars, custom license, created 2021; npm `@amcharts/amcharts5` 1,617,623 downloads last month — GitHub and npm APIs, 2026-10-10

### Inferences
- The logo is the whole enforcement mechanism: the free build is identical to the paid build, so the "license key" (`am5.addLicense(...)` in code, per general knowledge; not confirmed on the fetched pages) only suppresses the branding. Compliance is effectively self-selected by whoever is bothered by a logo.
- The $80/yr Basic tier is a very low entry point that captures hobby/public-website buyers, while the SaaS tier at $280/yr is the realistic price for a logged-in product.
- The fact that amCharts sells OEM at 4-5x the SaaS price per seat (rather than per-customer royalties) is a simple model a solo vendor could copy.

### Gaps
- The exact `addLicense` API docs page was not fetched (404 on guessed URLs); the mechanism is from the npm notice and reseller text.
- Whether the free (logo) version is formally permitted for commercial use is not stated on the download page; the freeware license text was not retrieved.
- No revenue or customer count signals were found.

## Key question 3: Tiptap

### Takeaway
Tiptap's editor core is MIT and in January 2026 it moved the original paid extensions (Details, Emoji, UniqueID) to MIT as well; what remains paid are cloud-backed features (Comments, Snapshots, AI, conversion) delivered through a private npm registry and priced as a platform subscription by cloud document count and developer seats ($49-$999/month annual). It is a VC-seeded (YC S23, $2.6M) Berlin company of roughly 18 people.

### Cited Findings
- "The Tiptap Editor is open source and free. Only platform features and cloud documents are priced." (MIT) — [Tiptap pricing](https://tiptap.dev/pricing)
- Plans (checked 2026-10-10): Start $59/mo or $49/mo annual (500 cloud docs per environment, 2 developer licenses, 2 environments); Team $179 / $149 (5,000 docs, 5 devs, 3 envs); Business $1,199 / $999 (50,000 docs, 10 devs, 5 envs, 99.9% uptime); Enterprise custom (on-prem, BYO auth/storage/AI models, SOC 2 Type II report) — [Tiptap pricing](https://tiptap.dev/pricing)
- Extra developer licenses $49/dev/month (monthly) or $39/dev/month (annual); AI Toolkit and Tracked Changes are custom-priced add-ons; cloud document add-on packs raise limits to 50k/200k/500k — [Tiptap pricing](https://tiptap.dev/pricing)
- Only documents stored in Tiptap Cloud count toward limits; exceeding the limit does not block, you get an email and the plan is auto-upgraded if you do not respond; no refunds; 30-day free trial without credit card on Start and Team — [Tiptap pricing](https://tiptap.dev/pricing)
- Q2-2025 archived pricing listed a Start tier with a $0 monthly placeholder and said "Your trial will expire after 30 days or when you want to use Tiptap for production outside of your staging environment" and "after the trial you must upgrade, or the paid plan starts automatically"; the third tier was then called Growth ($11,988/yr) — [Tiptap archive pricing Q2-25](https://tiptap.dev/archive/pricing-q2-25); a 2026 DEV post says the free plan was removed in June 2025 — [DEV: Tiptap pricing explained 2026](https://dev.to/eddyter/tiptap-pricing-explained-2026-free-vs-pro-vs-cloud-cost-breakdown-3l5g)
- Pro extensions install from the private registry `registry.tiptap.dev` using a personal `TIPTAP_PRO_TOKEN` in `.npmrc`/`.yarnrc.yml`; tokens are unique per user and do not expire ("treat them like passwords"; create a dedicated CI/CD user); some extensions (Snapshots, Comments, AI Toolkit features) require an active subscription — [Tiptap Pro extensions guide](https://tiptap.dev/docs/guides/pro-extensions)
- Original Pro announcement: subscribers get "access to the private registry to use all our Tiptap Pro extensions" and "full access to the documentation, the repository and the packages" as "a way to support our work" — [Tiptap Pro release notes](https://tiptap.dev/blog/release-notes/tiptap-pro)
- Community reaction (GitHub discussion #2653, opened 2022-03-27): a user called Tiptap Pro at €29/month (€348/yr) too expensive for three extensions (Details, Emoji, UniqueID) and asked for a lifetime license (6 votes); on 2026-01-16 a maintainer replied those three extensions are now MIT; on 2026-01-24 maintainer bdbch said many extensions moved to the MIT base and the remaining Pro extensions are tied to services (Comments, Snapshots, AI); the discussion was closed 2026-01-29 as outdated; another commenter complained they only need DOCX export but must pay for five seats because CI/CD counts as a developer seat — [GitHub ueberdosis/tiptap discussion 2653](https://github.com/ueberdosis/tiptap/discussions/2653)
- Install friction example: a developer hit an npm 404 for `@tiptap-pro/extension-emoji` without registry access and a collaborator removed Pro extensions from package.json so installs would not break — [Wappler community thread](https://community.wappler.io/t/call-for-help-building-tiptap-custom-app-connect-extension/58383)
- Funding: $2.6M Series Seed announced 2023-10-19, led by Expa with Y Combinator, Dennis Just, Radu Spineanu and Liz Wessel; "more than 2 million downloads per month"; customers named Substack, AxiosHQ, GitLab; post signed by six people — [Tiptap seed announcement](https://tiptap.dev/blog/tiptap-seriesseed); CB Insights lists an earlier $0.5M YC convertible note (Jan 2023) — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/tiptap/financials)
- YC directory: founded 2022, batch S23, team size 18, Berlin — [YC Tiptap jobs page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/tiptap/jobs)
- GitHub ueberdosis/tiptap: 38,673 stars, MIT; npm `@tiptap/core` 91,948,386 downloads last month (21,877,495 last week), `@tiptap/react` 74,989,379 last month — GitHub and npm APIs, 2026-10-10

### Inferences
- Tiptap's trajectory is a cautionary tale for "paid extensions behind a private registry": the model generated public complaints (price vs. three small extensions, CI seats counting as developers, broken installs), and within ~3.5 years the company open-sourced those extensions and pivoted the paywall to things that are inherently server-side (collaboration documents, comments, snapshots, AI, conversions). The private registry survives, but as the delivery channel for service-coupled SDKs, not as a gate on client-only code.
- Per-seat developer licensing is still used, layered under a usage metric (cloud documents), which is a SaaS-platform pricing structure rather than a component-license structure.
- No revenue is public; a ~18-person VC-seeded company at these price points implies a goal of hundreds-to-thousands of paying teams, not a solo-scale business.

### Gaps
- No Hacker News thread about Tiptap Pro pricing was found in search results.
- No revenue, ARR, or customer-count figures beyond the 2023 announcement.
- The current Pro-extension license terms (can you bundle/redistribute Pro packages in your own product?) were not stated on the fetched docs page.

## Key question 4: xyflow / React Flow Pro

### Takeaway
React Flow stays fully MIT; "Pro" is a subscription ($169 or $289 per month, Enterprise by quote) that buys Pro examples, prioritized issues, support, and the social permission to remove an on-canvas attribution link. The founders have written that GitHub Sponsors "didn't pay the bills, not even close," and that adding a SaaS-style pricing page plus the attribution made them "ramen profitable" within a few months, without ever publishing revenue numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Pro pricing (checked 2026-10-10): Starter $169/month (Pro Examples and Templates, prioritized GitHub issues, 1 team member, intro call); Professional $289/month (adds 5 team members, up to 1 hour email support per month); Enterprise quote (perpetual access to Pro Examples, 10 team members, 1 hour support, custom procurement); a yearly toggle exists but yearly prices were not rendered — [React Flow Pro](https://reactflow.dev/pro)
- "React Flow is open-source MIT-licensed software and will remain so"; subscriptions "fund ongoing maintenance, documentation, and community support" — [React Flow Pro](https://reactflow.dev/pro)
- Attribution: removing it requires a Pro subscription; "Only remove this attribution, if you are subscribed to React Flow Pro"; the page lists Starter $169/month and Professional $289/month — [React Flow Attribution](https://reactflow.dev/attribution)
- README policy: individuals on personal projects need not pay; "organizations making money from React Flow or Svelte Flow are asked for support" via React Flow Pro or GitHub Sponsors; libraries remain MIT — [xyflow GitHub README](https://github.com/xyflow/xyflow)
- Founder essay (2023-02-27): GitHub Sponsors covered only a small fraction of costs; they switched from donations to a "Thin-Crust Open Core" model (library stays MIT, a small paid "crust" of Pro examples and support); launched a four-tier Free/Starter/Pro/Enterprise pricing page in a "three-column SaaS-style layout" aimed at organizations so an engineer can make a budget case; added a small attribution link in the viewport corner that is removable under MIT but creates "a moment of friction"; paying customers arrived faster and paid more than sponsors; they became "ramen profitable" within a few months; subscribers said they were paying for the whole library, not the add-ons; feared service expectations did not materialize; caveats: existing agency business, two years of prior work, and a niche (node-based apps) where visible attribution works — [xyflow: Dear Open Source, let's do a better job of asking for money](https://xyflow.com/blog/asking-for-money-for-open-source)
- Timeline: React Flow 10 and React Flow Pro released 2022-03-15 (post cites "100k+ weekly downloads on npm" then); React Flow 11 post (2022-09-28) says "We got our first pro subscribers"; Pro Platform rebuilt and open-sourced 2023-12-06 (Pro Examples themselves stay in a private repo); Svelte Flow Pro launched 2026-06-15, Pro content moved into the main doc sites and the separate Pro Platform repo archived; the June 2026 post says React Flow Pro has been paid "for a few years now" and reports no pricing changes — [xyflow blog index](https://xyflow.com/blog); [Pro Platform open source post](https://xyflow.com/blog/react-flow-pro-platform-open-source); [Svelte Flow Pro post](https://xyflow.com/blog/svelte-flow-pro-and-more)
- The March 2023 team update mentions refunding a paid subscription from an organization they declined to serve, but gives no revenue or subscriber numbers — [xyflow spring update 2023](https://xyflow.com/blog/spring-update-2023)
- Team: full-time on React Flow and Svelte Flow, "Moritz, Hayleigh, Christopher, John and Peter", Berlin — [GitHub Sponsors xyflow](https://github.com/sponsors/xyflow)
- GitHub xyflow/xyflow: 38,624 stars, MIT, created 2019; npm `@xyflow/react` 56,689,918 downloads last month (13,381,777 last week) — GitHub and npm APIs, 2026-10-10

### Inferences
- The model is "sell permission and goodwill, not code": nothing a Pro subscriber receives is technically necessary, and enforcement is the visible attribution plus social norms. It works because the library's users are disproportionately funded startups building node-editor products that cannot ship a competitor's logo.
- Prices ($2,028-$3,468 per year) are set at B2B-SaaS levels and positioned per organization rather than per developer, which keeps procurement simple.
- Public revenue transparency is limited to qualitative statements ("ramen profitable" for a then ~3-5 person team); no ARR, subscriber count or churn data exists publicly.
