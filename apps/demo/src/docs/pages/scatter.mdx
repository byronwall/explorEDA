## Selection and row scope

Click a point to filter linked views to its row; click it again, or click empty plot space, to clear. Drag across the plot to brush a rectangular range on both axes. The brush filters linked views. Other active filters also limit the visible rows. The axes keep their domains from the full source population, so the scale stays stable as rows are filtered.

<Callout title="Trace a point.">
  Use the chart trace action, Alt-click a point, or right-click it and choose
  Trace this point to inspect its source values, calculations, filters, scales,
  and position. Trace works on every chart type except 3D scatter plots, data
  tables, pivot tables, summary tables, and notes.
</Callout>

## Settings and limits

Choose X, Y, and optional color fields. Change the color field from the label above the plot without opening settings, or right-click the plot and choose **Color by…**. Right-click an axis title to change the X or Y field the same way. Adjust point size and opacity to make overlap easier to see.

- On numeric axes, rows with missing or non-finite values have no point. Categorical axes can include a missing-value category.
- Canvas draws the points; SVG draws the axes and the brush.
- The rectangular brush selects ranges on both axes.

## Fits

The **Fit** tab draws a regression for each color group in each facet: linear, polynomial, or LOESS, with an optional overall fit through every group.

<LiveExample
  id="scatter-regression"
  caption="A linear fit per species, from the scatter regression example."
/>

A line or polynomial label states its equation with about three significant digits. When X sits far from zero, as with timestamps or positions measured from a distant origin, an equation in plain powers of x would need far more digits than that to reproduce the curve. The label then writes the equation around a round center near the mean X, such as `y = 0.0346(x − 1,000,000,000)² + 0.235(x − 1,000,000,000) + 1.23`, so the rounded numbers still give the drawn curve. The fit's trace lists the center c and the coefficients of each power of x − c, and a line shows Y at c in place of the offset at X = 0. Changing units, such as millimetres to metres, moves the center and rescales the coefficients but keeps the same curve.

## Connected points

Turn on **Connect points** to join points with straight lines in the order of a field, such as Year. The path keeps that order even when it doubles back along X, which a line chart would sort away.

- **Order by** sorts each path. Without a field, rows join in source order.
- **One path per** draws a path for each value, such as a country or an occupation. With Facets, each panel draws its own paths.
- **Path labels** names each path at its last point, or at both ends for a slope chart. Labels that would overlap move apart, with a thin leader back to their point.
- Each segment takes the color of the point it starts from, so a color field carries along the path.
- A row that another chart's filter removes, or that has no position on an axis, breaks the path instead of being skipped. Rows with no order value join no path.
- Connected points on a categorical axis sit at the center of their band instead of jittering, so a path over a category axis draws a dumbbell.
- Alt-click a path to list its rows in order, with each break and its reason. Alt-click a point to trace that row.

See [driving against gas prices](/examples/driving-scatter), [tax revenue as a slope chart](/examples/tax-slope-scatter), [party shares as dumbbells](/examples/pew-meaning-scatter), and [occupations in group panels](/examples/middle-class-jobs-scatter).

## Annotations

The **Notes** tab adds annotations that save with the chart.

- A **callout** is a label on one record. It finds the record by a key field, such as `Day`, whose values identify every row once. Reordered or replaced rows keep the callout on the same record. If no row has the key, or more than one does, the list says so and the chart draws nothing for it.
- A callout can instead sit at a **fixed point**, given in X and Y values. **Arrowhead** ends its leader line with an arrow at the subject.
- A **line** marks a fixed X or Y value, such as a target, and a **band** shades or brackets a span between two values. Filters never move them.
- A callout set to **Extreme** follows whichever row has the largest or smallest value, so its subject can change as filters change.
- A **region** is a rectangle or ellipse in X and Y values. It means "this area": filtering changes which points fall inside it.
- **Saved records** copies the rows your current selection holds, by key. It means "these records": the squares stay on the same rows when the selection changes, and the label counts how many show.
- A line's **Value from** can be **Calculated**: the mean, median, minimum, or maximum of the axis field over **All rows** (every row the chart can draw) or **Filtered rows** (those that pass the current filters). Its label names the rows and shows the result.
- Each annotation has a name, and **Duplicate** copies it below.

Labels for lines, bands, and new callouts take the clearest spot near what they describe, away from points, other labels, and the plot edge. A callout with an offset in Dashboard text, or one you have dragged, stays where it was put, and the other labels make room for it. Drag a callout's label, or focus it and use the arrow keys, to move the label without moving its record. Each drag is one undo step, and Escape puts the label back. When filters leave out a callout's record, the callout hides and the list marks it **Filtered out** until the record returns. An annotation past the axis range stays in the list as **Outside the view**.

The bubble scatter example has a callout on day 31, a conversion target, a mean response time over filtered rows, and a region around the fastest days.
